How to get your bank transactions into Notion automatically

A Notion database is a good home for your spending: your own columns, your own views, your notes next to every payment. Getting the transactions in is the hard part. This guide covers the free way, automation services for the banks that have them, and a sync that runs on its own for banks in Europe, the US and Canada.

I'm David Alves, and I make Klartion, one of the ways described here. The free way comes first, because for many people it's all they need.

The short answer

  • You're happy to do it once or twice a month: download a CSV file from your online banking and merge it into your Notion database. It's free.
  • Your bank is on Zapier: a few banks, such as Mercury and bunq, have a trigger there for new transactions that can add a row to Notion.
  • You want it hands off: Klartion syncs 2,500+ European banks and most major US and Canadian banks into Notion on a schedule. €45 once, free for 7 days.

The free way: Notion's CSV import

Notion turns a CSV file into database rows: each row becomes an item and each column becomes a property. You can create a new database from the file, or add the file to a database you already have. Everything below is from Notion's import help page.

  1. Download your transactions as CSV

    In your online banking, open the account's transaction list and look for an export or download option. Pick CSV and the dates you want. If your bank only offers an Excel file, open it and save it as .csv first.

  2. Tidy the file

    Notion's help asks for a few things:

    • The first row holds the column names, and every column has one.
    • When you merge into an existing database, the column names match the database's property names exactly.
    • Dates are in MM/DD/YYYY format. If your bank exports the day first, change those.
    • Amounts with currency symbols, percent signs or minus signs may import incorrectly. Notion suggests mapping such a column to Text, but Text can't be totaled, so it's usually better to remove the symbols in the file and keep plain numbers.
    • If you see garbled characters, save the file again as UTF-8.

  3. The first time: create the database from the file

    Import the CSV into Notion and choose to create a new database. You map each CSV column to the property you want, so Date can be a date and Amount a number.

  4. Every time after: Merge with CSV

    Open the database as a full page, click the ••• menu at the top and choose Merge with CSV. Match the CSV's columns to your properties and the new rows appear.

What to watch for

  • Merging only adds rows. Notion doesn't update rows that are already there, so if two downloads overlap by a few days, those transactions show up twice. Download from the day after your last import.
  • Pending payments. If your bank's export includes payments that haven't settled yet, they won't change in Notion when they do. Leaving out the last few days avoids that.
  • File size. Notion accepts up to 5 MB per file on the Free plan and 50 MB on paid plans.
  • One file per account. Each bank account is its own download, so add a column for the account name if you merge several into one database.

It costs nothing, and it works with any bank, anywhere, as long as it lets you download a CSV.

Automation services

Services like Zapier connect apps with triggers and actions. Notion's action there is Create Data Source Item, which adds a row to a database. What matters is whether your bank has a trigger. A few do. For example, on Zapier:

  • Mercury (US business banking) has a New Transaction trigger. Zapier notes that it fires before the transaction settles.
  • bunq has New Payment and New Card Payment triggers.

Accounting tools that already hold your bank feed, such as Xero, have Zapier connections to Notion too. To check your own bank, search for it on Zapier's app directory, and look at Zapier's pricing page for what your number of transactions would cost each month. If yours isn't there, read on.

A sync that runs on its own: Klartion

Klartion connects to your bank with read-only access and writes every new transaction into a Notion database, on a schedule you pick. It runs in Docker on your own computer, NAS or server, so your transactions go from the bank to your Notion without passing through Klartion's servers.

  • Europe: 2,500+ banks in 29 countries, through Enable Banking, which is free. European banks ask you to renew access every 90 to 180 days, and Klartion emails you before it runs out.
  • US and Canada: most major banks, such as Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, RBC and TD, through SimpleFIN Bridge, which costs $15 a year, paid to SimpleFIN. Check your bank is on SimpleFIN's list before you pay. Some banks have known quirks.

  1. Start it

    Download one small compose file and run docker compose up -d. The setup guide has the commands for Mac, Linux and Windows, and covers the Enable Banking or SimpleFIN sign-up too. It takes about 15 minutes.

  2. Connect Notion

    In Klartion's setup, click Connect Notion, sign in and click Allow, then type the code Notion's tab shows you. Klartion creates its database with all the columns. You can use a database of your own instead.

  3. Connect your bank

    Pick your bank and approve read-only access on its own login page, or paste your SimpleFIN setup token. Choose the date to start from, and the first sync fills the database.

Questions

Can Notion import a bank statement for free?

Yes, as a CSV file. Download the transactions from your online banking as CSV, then use Merge with CSV from the menu of a full-page database. A merge only adds rows, so import each period once to avoid duplicates.

Is it safe to connect my bank to Notion?

With Klartion, access is read-only and goes through Enable Banking or SimpleFIN Bridge. Klartion never sees your bank password and can't make payments. It runs on your machine and writes straight to your Notion, and the code is open for anyone to read.

Can I use my own Notion database?

Yes. Choose Use an integration key instead in the Notion step and paste your integration key and the database's address. Klartion adds the columns it needs and renames the title column to Merchant. Existing columns of the wrong type are reported, never changed.

My bank isn't in Europe, the US or Canada. What can I do?

Klartion only covers those regions. The CSV import above works with any bank that lets you download your transactions.

Do I need Docker?

Yes. Klartion runs in Docker, which is free and installs on a Mac, a Windows PC, a Linux machine, a NAS or a Raspberry Pi. It isn't offered as a hosted service. The setup guide walks you through every step.

What if Klartion's license server can't be reached?

Klartion keeps syncing for 30 days after the license server last confirmed your key, and the Status page shows the days left. Once it can reach the server again, the 30 days start over.

What happens when the trial ends?

Nothing is deleted, and your Notion database stays exactly as it is. Syncing pauses until you add a license.
